# What This Thing Is — In Plain Words

*(The demo you clicked around in is a pretend version — like a model of a house before it's built. Nothing in it is real yet. But everything it does, the real one will do.)*

---

## The one-sentence version

**Customers order. The computer writes your roast list. You roast what the list says. That's it.**

---

## The cookie jar rule (why you'll never oversell again)

Picture a cookie jar with 240 cookies. Every time someone buys one, the jar counts down: 239, 238, 237...

When the jar hits **zero**, the "buy" button turns off. Instantly. By itself.

Nobody can buy a cookie that isn't there — the computer physically won't let it happen. Not "usually won't." **Can't.** So when 800 people rush your drop at 9:00am, you sell every single bag and not one bag more.

## The waitlist waves (no more stampede)

Instead of announcing to everyone at once, the system texts your waitlist in groups:

- 9:00am — first 200 people get the link
- 9:20am — next 200
- 9:40am — everyone else

And there's a **2-bag limit per person**, so the fast people can't clean you out and nobody gets scalped.

## The sheet (your new morning)

Every night, the computer adds up every bag people ordered. Then it does one piece of math:

> **236 bags ordered ÷ 21 bags per roast = 12 roasts**

And it writes your list for the day: *"Roast #1: Camacho. Roast #2: Camacho..."* You come in, look at the sheet, roast what it says, tap **Done** after each one. No adding things up in your head. No guessing. No Sharpie.

## The labels (why the date is already printed)

Because the sheet decides **ahead of time** which day each coffee gets roasted, every label can be printed early — with the right roast date already on it. A thousand bags, zero hand-writing.

## The inbox helper

When customers text or email — "where's my order?", "I moved, new address!" — the computer writes a reply that sounds like you. You read it and tap **Yes, send** (or fix a word first). If someone changed their address, the shipping label fixes itself too. What used to eat two hours takes about four minutes.

---

## The two screens

- **The Storefront** — what your customers see. The shop.
- **Drop Control** — what you see. The sheet, the drop, the labels, the inbox.

They share one brain. When a customer buys a bag, your sheet already knows.

**You roast. The system does everything else.**
