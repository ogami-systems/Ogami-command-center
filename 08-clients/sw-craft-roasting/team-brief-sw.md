# S&W Craft Roasting — Client Brief

**For Nathan & Daniel · from Michael · July 2026 · Internal**

## Context

Nick owns and operates S&W Craft Roasting: one 12&nbsp;kg drum roaster (~21 one-lb bags per batch), single-origin light roasts, sold through Square. His coffee sells itself — every "drop" sells out within hours, and waitlists run 150–450 people per coffee. The demand is not the problem. The operation behind it is: it runs on memory, Sharpies, and heroic effort.

## The problem (his words, our map)

- **Oversells.** Drops move faster than his inventory tracking — he sells bags that don't exist, then spends days on refunds and apologies.
- **The stampede.** He can't announce a drop without hundreds hitting the store at once.
- **Hand-written roast dates** on every bag — ~1,000 bags per drop.
- **Packing takes 3–4 days** per 1,000-bag drop.
- **The inbox** — tracking, address changes, refunds — quietly eats hours weekly.

All five are handoff leaks, straight off the anatomy map: money (oversell refunds, missed sales), time (packing, Sharpie, inbox), quality (promises lost between channels).

## The solution — "Roastery OS"

One system: **orders are the input, the roast sheet is the output.** Five modules:

| Module | What it does | Leak it seals |
|---|---|---|
| Storefront | Atomic stock ledger — oversell is impossible; 2-bag caps; instant sold-out | Money |
| Wave texts | Waitlist released in timed groups (200 at 9:00, 200 at 9:20…) | The stampede |
| Roast Brain | Orders ÷ 21 bags = batches, auto-scheduled onto roast days; Nick roasts what the sheet says | Time / quality |
| Fulfillment | Pick lists in roast order, labels **pre-printed with declared roast dates**, one-click postage — packing 3–4 days → ~1.5 | Time |
| Inbox Agent | Drafted replies in Nick's voice, approve-to-send (Phase 3 upsell) | Time |

## Value

**To Nick:** every bag sold, zero oversold; ~15 hrs/week back (packing alone: ~18 hrs/drop); drops without dread. **To Ogami:** first flagship build; the demo's four core functions (demand, batching, scheduling, atomic reserve) become our reusable commerce pattern; a measurable case study — and specialty coffee talks to specialty coffee.
